How to Convert a Primavera P6 XER File to Excel

A practical, no-fluff guide for planning engineers: what an XER file actually is, the manual copy-paste method (and why it breaks down), and how to auto-generate a full 22-sheet analytics workbook — 100% offline.

What is an XER file?

An .xer file is the native project-exchange export of Oracle Primavera P6. A single XER holds the entire schedule — activities, durations, logic relationships, WBS structure, calendars, resources and costs — stored as tab-delimited text in Primavera's own proprietary, multi-table relational format. By default it can only be read back into Primavera P6.

That is exactly why "convert P6 XER to Excel" is one of the most common searches on the planning desk: clients, executives and reviewers rarely have a P6 licence, but everyone can open Excel.

Can you just open an XER file in Excel?

Technically yes — and it is almost always useless. Because an XER is tab-delimited text, you can rename it to .txt and import it into Excel. What you get is dozens of stacked raw tables (PROJ, TASK, TASKPRED, PROJWBS, CALENDAR…) full of coded internal IDs, with none of the relationships resolved. You cannot read the schedule, trace logic, or build a report from it.

To get a usable Excel file, the XER has to be parsed: the activity IDs joined to descriptions, WBS paths rebuilt, calendars applied, and float / dates calculated. That is the real work — and it is where your method choice matters.

Method 1 — Manual copy-paste from P6

If you have a P6 licence, the traditional route is:

  1. Open the project in Primavera P6.
  2. Lay out the Activity table columns you need (ID, description, dates, durations, total float, % complete, cost).
  3. Select all rows, copy, and paste into Excel.
  4. Reformat headers, fix date formats, add S-curves and pivot summaries by hand.

The limitation: it only exports the columns currently on screen, it strips all logic and WBS hierarchy, and every reporting cycle you repeat the same 2–4 hours of formatting. On a 2,000-activity schedule with weekly updates, this is the single biggest time sink on the planning desk.

Method 2 — Generic online XER-to-XLSX converters

Free web converters will turn an XER into a raw XLSX table dump. They are fine for a quick look, but they carry two serious problems for professional use:

  • Confidentiality: your schedule is uploaded to a third-party server. On EPC, oil & gas, defence and government projects this usually breaches the client NDA.
  • No analytics: you get flat tables, not an executive reporting pack — no S-curves, no EVM, no schedule-quality checks, no dashboard.

Frequently asked questions

Can I open an XER file directly in Excel?

Not usefully. It opens as raw, coded table dumps with unresolved relationships — not a readable schedule. You need a parser that rebuilds the activity, WBS and logic links.

Do I need a Primavera P6 licence to convert an XER file?

No. Once you have the exported .xer or .xml, Schedulytics reads it with its own built-in CPM engine — no active P6 licence required.

What about XML (PMXML) files?

The same applies. Schedulytics reads both P6 XER and XML exports and produces the identical Excel, HTML and Word/PDF outputs.
